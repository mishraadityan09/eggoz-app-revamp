01 · Summary

A strong product held back by its first 10 seconds

The app is built on a modern React Native stack and has great brand content. But the moment a new customer opens it — the launch, the hero carousel, the first scroll — is slow and janky, and the paths to ordering (location, Add, Subscribe, Cart) all have avoidable friction. Most of the fixes are incremental; no rewrite of the backend is needed.

What's working well

  • Modern foundation. Hermes, Reanimated and react-native-screens are already in place, so improvements can ship screen by screen.
  • Strong brand. Warm palette, good product photography, recognisable ambassador campaign.
  • Good trust story on the product page: nutrition facts, local farms, "11 safety checks".
  • Clear value proposition: daily subscription, morning delivery, coins and wallet rewards.
  • Feed scrolling after load is smooth (3–4% janky frames), proving the stack can perform.

What's holding it back

  • Slow, janky first impression. ~9 s to home content; the hero carousel drops ~47% of frames.
  • Loading looks broken. A blank white screen, then grey boxes and buttons that change size.
  • Location can dead-end. If location fails, "Try Again" is the only option.
  • Login before anything. Add, Subscribe, Cart and Profile all require login first.
  • Subscription is hidden behind an unlabelled calendar icon, and the product page has no sticky Add bar.

03 · Performance
P1 · High

The hero carousel is the first thing customers see, and the least smooth part of the app

P1
The carousel auto-advances and takes up the most prominent area of home.

What was measured

  • With the carousel auto-playing and untouched, 47% of frames were janky. The median frame took 32 ms, which is about 30 fps instead of 60.
  • Swiping it by hand gave the same result (47% janky), even though the banners had already loaded.
  • For comparison, scrolling the product feed once loaded is smooth (3–4% janky). The problem is concentrated in the carousel and in first load, not in the whole app.
  • Android's frame statistics show many slow UI-thread frames during auto-play (85 of 303) and occasional slow bitmap uploads.

Why it matters

The carousel carries the offers ("₹250 extra", "5% off on subscription") and is on screen every time the app opens. A stuttering hero makes the whole app feel slow, even where it isn't.

Likely causes (to confirm with source access)

  • The active-slide / dot index is held in React state high in the home tree, so each auto-advance re-renders the home screen.
  • Banner images are larger than their on-screen size and get decoded or re-uploaded as slides change.
  • Looping or peeking slides render extra full-size images during the transition.
  • Auto-play starts while the rest of home is still mounting and fetching.

Recommended fix

  • Drive the slide position and pagination dots from a Reanimated shared value, e.g. react-native-reanimated-carousel or a paged FlatList. That means zero React re-renders per slide.
  • Move the carousel into its own memoised component so home never re-renders because of it.
  • Serve banners at display size (about 1080 px wide, WebP/AVIF, under 150 KB) through CDN resizing.
  • Render with expo-image: memory and disk cache, a blur-hash placeholder, and prefetching of the next slide.
  • Start auto-play only after home's first render, and pause it when off-screen or when the app is in the background.
  • Use a fixed aspect-ratio container so nothing shifts when a banner arrives.

Target: under 5% janky frames and no frame over 100 ms, verified with the same scripted test.

03 · Performance

The first 10 seconds: slow launch, blank screen, shifting layout

P3
1. About 4 s after re-opening the app from the launcher: a completely blank white screen.
P3
2. Then: no banner, grey boxes instead of products, and the Add and calendar buttons still changing width.
3. Final layout. Every element moved or appeared between frame 2 and frame 3.
P2 · HighAbout 9 s from tapping the icon to home content

Measured at 8.6 s, 9.6 s and 9.6 s across three cold launches, including the branded splash. Google's Android vitals treat a cold start of 5 s or more as excessive.

P3 · HighBlank screen, then content that jumps into place

There is no skeleton with final dimensions, so images, badges (the coin icon) and buttons appear at different times and push the layout around.

P4 · MediumThe first scroll after launch stutters

30–54% janky frames on the first scroll, with worst frames of 101–300 ms. The same scroll is smooth (3–4%) once everything has loaded, which points to work happening at mount time: data arriving, images decoding and lists rendering.

Recommended fix

  • Cache-first home. Persist the last home response (TanStack Query + MMKV) and render it instantly, then refresh in the background.
  • Keep the native splash (react-native-bootsplash / expo-splash-screen) until that first render, so there is never a blank white screen.
  • Skeletons with exact final sizes for banner, cards and badges, so there is zero layout shift.
  • Home as one virtualised FlashList, with sections as item types and horizontal FlashLists for rails, plus memoised cards.
  • Trim startup. Lazy-load non-home screens, defer SDK and analytics init, and fetch wallet and coins after first paint.
  • Measure continuously. Firebase Performance or Sentry traces in production, plus an automated Flashlight / Maestro run in CI.

04 · UX · First launch

Location: two system dialogs, then a possible dead end

U2
1. The system permission dialog appears straight after "Skip", with no explanation from the app.
U2
2. A second dialog follows, asking the user to turn on Google Location Accuracy.
U1
3. After "No thanks": "Location unavailable", with "Try Again" as the only action.
U1 · HighA location failure blocks the whole app

There is no way to enter a pincode or address, pick a saved address or browse products. Anyone who declines a prompt, is indoors with weak GPS or is ordering for a different address is stuck.

Offer "Enter pincode / search address" (Places autocomplete) and "Browse products", fall back to approximate location, and remember the chosen address.

U2 · MediumPermission is requested before the user knows why

Two system dialogs appear back to back on first use. Unexplained prompts are more likely to be declined, and on Android a second refusal blocks the prompt permanently.

Show a short in-app sheet first ("We use your location to check delivery to your area"), with "Use current location" and "Enter pincode". Only request Location Accuracy if a precise fix actually fails.

04 · UX · Ordering & accessibility

Guests can browse, but can't start an order

U3
Add, Subscribe, Cart and Profile all open this sheet for guests.
U14
Search: the chip icons are empty grey circles.
U3 · HighLogin is required before adding to cart

The app offers "Skip" but then blocks every purchase action. That means asking for a phone number and an OTP at the moment of highest intent, before the customer has even built a cart.

Keep a guest cart on the device, ask for login at checkout, auto-read the OTP (Android SMS Retriever) and offer Phone Number Hint so the number can be filled in one tap.

U14 · LowSearch empty state

The chip icons render as empty circles, and most of the screen is unused.

Show recent searches and best sellers, and give results instantly as the user types.

U16 · MediumMissing screen-reader labels

5 of the 18 tappable elements on home have no text or label: all three carousel banners and both calendar (subscribe) buttons. TalkBack users can't tell what they do.

Add accessibilityLabel and accessibilityRole to icon buttons and banners.

U15 · Medium

Text contrast is below WCAG AA (4.5 : 1) in 5 of 6 samples

Colours sampled directly from screenshot pixels. The brand orange can stay as a fill colour; it just needs dark text, or a deeper shade wherever text sits on it or in it.
